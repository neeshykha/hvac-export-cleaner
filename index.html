HVAC Export Cleaner

A smart-thermostat export is thousands of sparse telemetry pings, and almost none of them say anything a person asked about. Drop one here and get back either the moments someone changed the setpoint, or a clean month-by-month timeline of temperature, state, and target.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
Drop one or more CSV exports here or click to choose files. Multiple exports get merged for the timeline view.

Only the rows where a target temperature was set. A row stays if tt has a value, or if the unit is in heat-cool mode (tm = 4) and holds a heat or cool setpoint instead. Values pass through exactly as exported.

Every ping, merged across files and sorted, with ambient temperature, current state, and target temperature carried forward so each row is a complete snapshot. Split by calendar month, not by file, because exports spill across month edges.

What the columns mean, and the rules this applies

The export has twelve columns: timestamp, cf, ch, cs, ct, ctt, fs, htt, internal_temp, tf, tm, tt. Most rows fill in one or two of them. The ones that matter here:

  • tt: target temperature, the setpoint in single-mode operation.
  • htt / ctt: heat and cool targets, used instead of tt in heat-cool mode.
  • tm: target mode, what the unit is set to. 0 off, 1 cool, 2 heat, 3 emergency heat, 4 heat-cool.
  • cs: current state, what the unit is actually doing. 0 off, 1 cooling, 2 heating, 3 emergency heat, 4 aux heat. Yes, 4 means something different here than in tm; that's the most common way these exports get misread.
  • ct: ambient temperature at the thermostat.

Quirks it handles: CRLF line endings (a stray \r makes the last column look filled on every row), tm written as 4.0, setpoint rows with a blank mode (kept, mode left blank), duplicate rows where two monthly exports overlap, and columns in any order. Readings of exactly 32.0°F are usually a sensor dropout; they're counted and flagged but left in, because silently editing telemetry is worse than showing a glitch.
